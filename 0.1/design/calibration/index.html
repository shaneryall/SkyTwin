    How skytwin-calibrate learns a profile¶

    Every number comes from measuring frames: real frames to set the profile, and simulated frames, measured with the same code, to check and correct it. Nothing about a particular instrument is built in. Its header names go in a keyword map; its measured profile stays with you.

    The steps for running it are in learn-a-profile.md, its commands in the calibrate reference, and every option in the command-line reference.

    The learn pipeline¶

    learn runs these steps in order:

    1. Headers: finds exposure, time, temperature (°C or K), sub-satellite point, Earth-limb angle, spacecraft position and velocity (and their frame, preferring the exposure start), pointing, roll, tracking rate, gain, read noise, binning, pixel size and pixel scale, by scoring every card on its name and its comment. The pixel size is taken as after binning (as XPIXSZ is) unless its comment says otherwise.
    2. Frames: keeps the most common size and binning; other modes are listed, to learn separately.
    3. Camera: gain, read noise, pixel scale and exposure from headers; leading columns that get no light; pixel type; the saturation level (where frame maxima pile up). Then the read-out chain: a serial lag (noise correlated along rows as aᵏ, sources trailing one way: noise.serial_lag), and, when the headers give no gain, gain and read noise by photon transfer from pairs of frames with the same exposure, taken up to 1.5 times the archive's usual spacing apart (5 to 60 s): the clipped block variance of the difference against level, over the central half of the frame, with the lag's variance reduction undone. On frames with the bias still in, the read noise comes from the darkest frames.
    4. Statistics: background, noise, ramps, pattern noise and sharp events per frame (measure). On frames whose bias sits below the clip, noise comes from quantiles above the clip, where the MAD would read 0. Rows the read-out corrupted (salt-and-pepper bands, dropped rows) are counted and kept out of the sharp events; each row's spread comes from neighbouring-pixel differences, so glare gradients and dense radiation storms are not mistaken for corruption. The odd/even row offset (even rows' level minus their odd neighbours') is measured per frame. The corrupted bands found (rows, and whether noise, dropped or saturated) become readout_faults.
    5. Detector: hot pixels from the median, over 150 frames, of each pixel's excess over its neighbours, and how their rates follow temperature, from the longest exposures that number at least 10 frames (a row's dark charge while it waits to be read does not scale with the exposure, so short frames would inflate the rates); the flat field (a circular field stop if there is one, vignetting inside it, and pixel response non-uniformity) from how each pixel follows the frame level; the PSF from frames whose stars measure round (with any serial lag undone first), picked from all the inputs by their header tracking rate; frame-transfer smear from the column excess above and below bright unsaturated stars (corrected against simulated frames with --skytwin).
    6. Pointing: when the frames carry their own celestial WCS (an archive's Astrometry.net solutions, say), at least 20 of them do, and --skytwin and a catalogue (--catalog or --ucac4) are given, stars are matched to the catalogue through it, and a joint fit over up to 80 frames (per frame an affine map of tangent-plane coordinates, shared radial distortion about the image centre) gives the pixel scale, parity, camera.distortion (kept when k1 is 5σ from zero) and the photometric zeropoint. This works where a fresh plate solve fails (fields tens of degrees wide, a few dozen bright stars). Otherwise, with --ucac4 (a CSV catalogue is not enough) and Fayde (pip install fayde): plate solves of the frames with round stars give the true pixel scale and parity, the roll keyword's convention, the pixel the header RA/Dec refers to, the header's pointing errors, and the photometric zeropoint (aperture photometry of the matched catalogue stars, corrected to total flux with the fitted PSF).
    7. Earth glare, with --skytwin: a tiny twin of each frame gives Skytwin's own geometry (angle from the limb, sunlit or dark limb, Earth's direction in the image); the background of sunlit-limb frames against angle gives the table, dark-limb frames the dark factor, and the frame ramps the gradient and its direction. Frames inside the glare zone leave the pedestal sampling so their glare is not counted twice. Then noise.smooth_field: smooth background structure with sources masked, from block-to-block covariances that hold no pixel noise. With --skytwin it is corrected against simulated frames, twins of the real sky when a catalogue and the frames' pointing are known (frames with their own WCS give it), since a real field's faint stars below the mask add to the covariance too.
    8. Header style, so simulated frames carry the same keywords: constant cards copied, computable ones templated.
    9. Profile blocks from the statistics (profile).
    10. With --skytwin: two refinement passes, then simulated twins and a realism report. Add --catalog or --ucac4 for twins of the real sky.

    Blocks fitted indirectly¶

    skytwin-calibrate learn fits most blocks, the read-out faults among them, directly from frame statistics. The blocks below are fitted indirectly.

    Background light, pedestal and fixed field¶

    skytwin-calibrate learn splits each frame's level into sky_e_per_s, the pedestal and noise.fixed_field when the flat has a field stop: block medians are fitted as pedestal + fixed field + light × flat, the fixed field kept orthogonal to a constant and to the flat's shape.

    Serial lag¶

    skytwin-calibrate learn takes a as the ratio of the lag-2 to the lag-1 correlation of the background along rows (white noise added after the filter lowers both alike), the side from where resolved stars carry more flux, and undoes the lag before choosing frames and fitting the PSF, so the PSF is the optical one.

    Smooth background field¶

    skytwin-calibrate learn measures noise.smooth_field with sources masked: block medians about a quadratic surface, whose covariances between neighbouring blocks (lags 1 and 2) hold no pixel noise. Their ratio gives the scale and their size the RMS, through a table made by measuring simulated fields the same way.

    Colour term¶

    skytwin-calibrate learn fits camera.color_term from forced photometry when its catalogue carries a colour (a Gaia CSV): per-star zeropoint against colour, clipped, kept when 5σ from zero.

    Choosing a catalogue¶

    The catalogue sets the photometry, so use one whose magnitudes hold for the stars the camera sees: UCAC4's own magnitudes saturate brighter than about 8, which a wide-field camera's stars often are. The zeropoint comes from forced photometry at every catalogue position (no detection bias), and learn warns when it drifts with magnitude. With a colour column (bp_rp) it also fits camera.color_term. Gaia DR3 lacks many stars brighter than G ~ 3, often the brightest in a wide field; add_bright_stars.py adds them to a Gaia CSV from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (VizieR V/50).

    Calibration frames¶

    With --calibration, learn also reads the camera's bias, dark and flat frames. They are sorted by their header frame type (IMAGETYP and kin), else by the folder or file name, and only those of the science frames' size and binning are used. What they measure replaces what the science frames would give:

    • gain by photon transfer from pairs of flats at the same exposure, with the read noise from pairs of bias frames;
    • the dark current from master darks of the longest and shortest exposure, so the bias and the charge a row collects while it waits to be read cancel (the part that does not grow with exposure is reported, not kept);
    • the radiation rate from hits in the darks, each dark less the median of its night and exposure, leaving out hits at pixels that fire in two or more darks (flickering pixels); with two or more exposures, the hits at zero exposure give the read-out time;
    • dust shadows from the master flat, found with ring-shaped matched filters and measured from their radial profiles, in chip pixels as Skytwin draws them.

    Hot pixels stay the ones the science frames show; the darks' own are the fallback when the science frames find none, and profile.learn.md compares the two.

    Frames whose stars trail¶

    When the stars in most sampled frames trail (a telescope tracking a satellite, say), learn measures each frame's trail length and angle from the frame's autocorrelation, which for segments of length L is a ridge whose profile along the trail is a triangle of half-width L. Then:

    • the PSF comes from the profile across isolated trails, which the motion does not blur; for a sum of round Gaussians the profile across is the same sum of one-dimensional Gaussians, so the fit gives camera.fwhm_px and psf.wings directly;
    • frames with their own WCS are matched by trail centres, found with a filter along the trail, and the zeropoint comes from boxes along the trails, corrected for the PSF halo outside the box and in its background ring;
    • radiation is counted outside the trails in frames of the longest exposure, leaving out events at a pixel that fires in two or more frames (hot and flickering pixels, a tracked target); the read-out time comes from how the hits split between the chip's halves, and the rate is an upper bound, since every other event counts too;
    • each frame's twin trails at that frame's rate, so the smooth-field correction and the realism report compare like with like.

    Ground frames¶

    Frames with an airmass, or the telescope's altitude, in their headers are taken from the ground. Their zeropoint is fitted against airmass, frame by frame, so the profile's zeropoint is the one above the atmosphere and its description gives the extinction a scene's ground site should use (a site applies the atmosphere itself). When the frames span too little airmass to fit it, --extinction (mag/airmass, 0.2 if not given) moves the zeropoint up from the frames' median airmass, and the report says it was assumed. Forced photometry uses an aperture of 2.5 times the PSF's FWHM, with the background 1.36 to 2 apertures out, so a wide PSF's wings do not land in the background. With plate solves, a --catalog (a Gaia extract, say) is measured through each solution, so the colour term and the extinction come from it rather than from UCAC4's magnitudes.

    The Cramér–Rao bound in scores¶

    Truth files of static frames (no tracking, no pointing motion) carry each star's Cramér–Rao bound (crlb_px, crlb_flux_e), from the actual PSF and per-pixel variance. A detector whose centroid errors have an RMS of 1 in units of the bound reaches the best precision any method can.
