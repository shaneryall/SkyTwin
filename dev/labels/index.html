    Training labels reference¶

    The label record in the truth file, the classes and boxes built from it, and the files skytwin-export writes for YOLO (Ultralytics) and COCO tools such as DETR. Export a training set gives the steps; design/labels.md explains how the signal-to-noise ratio is computed and why the rules below were chosen.

    Labels in the truth file¶

    Each star, streak and target in the truth file has a label that records what a detector could see of it.

    Field Meaning
    path Pixel positions along which the source was drawn, pointing motion included. A still source has one point. A moving one has the path from its first to its last lit point, simplified to within 0.5 px and to at most 65 points, with both ends kept. Empty when nothing was drawn.
    snr Signal-to-noise ratio of an ideal matched filter, √(Σ sᵢ² / vᵢ) over the source's pixels on the image, where sᵢ is its expected signal in electrons after the flat field and vᵢ the pixel's variance. No detector can do better.
    peak_snr Highest single-pixel signal-to-noise ratio, max sᵢ / √vᵢ.
    extent_px Distance from path of the farthest pixel centre where the source is at least the pixel's noise σ, in pixels.
    saturated Whether a pixel where the source is at least the noise σ saturates, counting everything on that pixel: it reaches full well, or the level where the delivered pixel clips at the output's maximum, whichever is lower.

    Classes¶

    skytwin.labels.objects reads the labels of a frame. Stars are class star. Targets and pixel streaks are class satellite. A source is a streak when the farthest two points of its path are more than twice the camera FWHM apart, and otherwise a point. With by_form, each class splits into star_point, star_streak, satellite_point and satellite_streak.

    Boxes¶

    The labelled region of an object is its path widened by a radius. The radius is extent_px or the FWHM, whichever is larger, plus 0.5 px to reach the edges of the outermost pixels. An object is labelled when its snr is at least min_snr, 5 by default.

    Skytwin puts pixel centres on whole numbers, while YOLO and COCO measure from the outer corner of the first pixel. Labels therefore add 0.5 px to each axis. x runs along a row of the image array and y down its rows. Images are written with array row 0 at the top, so a FITS viewer that draws row 0 at the bottom shows the same frame flipped vertically.

    Tiles¶

    A large frame can be cut into tiles. A point is kept in a tile when at least half of its box lies inside. A streak is kept when part of its path lies inside. Its signal-to-noise ratio in the tile is its full value times the square root of the share of its on-image length inside. Axis-aligned boxes are clamped to the tile. A streak's rotated box runs along its part in the tile from the first to the last point, with the half-width of its radius. Where it crosses the edge of the tile, the edge cuts it, so the box holds all of the streak's light inside the tile; a cut box has four corners but is no longer a rectangle (YOLO-OBB and COCO's rotated_box give the corners).

    Export options¶

    skytwin-export SCENE OUT_DIR takes these options:

    Option Effect Default
    --frames Frames to render the scene's batch.frames, else 1
    --start First frame index 0
    --seed Replaces the scene's seed the scene's
    --format yolo, yolo-obb or coco; repeat for several. yolo and yolo-obb both write labels/, so choose one. yolo
    --classes Kinds to label, separated by commas: star, satellite, or star,satellite star,satellite
    --by-form Split each kind into point and streak classes off
    --min-snr Least matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio in the tile 5
    --min-visible Least share of a point's box inside a tile 0.5
    --min-radius Least box half-width before the half pixel, px FWHM
    --streak-px Least span of a streak's path, px 2 × FWHM
    --tile, --overlap Tile size and least overlap, px; the overlap is at least 0 and below the tile size whole frame, 0
    --val Share of frames for validation 0.1
    --image asinh (8-bit) or raw16 (the delivered pixels in 16 bits) asinh
    --workers Render threads every core
    --keep-truth Also write each frame's truth file to truth/ off
    --overwrite Replace an earlier export in the output folder; without it, a folder that holds one is refused off

    Frame k is the scene's frame k, with seed + k, so a set can be rebuilt or extended exactly. The last --val share of the frames forms the validation split, and all tiles of a frame stay in its split.

    Output files¶

    The output folder holds:

    images/{train,val}/000123.png              000123_04.png for tile 4
labels/{train,val}/000123.txt              YOLO or YOLO-OBB
data.yaml                                  Ultralytics dataset file
annotations/instances_{train,val}.json     COCO
skytwin.json                               scene, options and counts

    YOLO label lines are class cx cy w h, and YOLO-OBB lines are class x1 y1 x2 y2 x3 y3 x4 y4, all as fractions of the tile size. COCO category ids start at 1, image file names are relative to images/<split>/, and each annotation carries a skytwin record with the form, signal-to-noise ratio, saturation, rotated box and the source's place in the truth file. COCOeval keeps 100 detections per image by default, and a tile of stars can hold more. Raise params.maxDets when stars are a class.

    Image stretch¶

    The asinh image measures each pixel in units of the frame's noise, z = (image − median) / σ, with σ = 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation. z is clipped to [−2, 1000] and mapped through asinh(z / 3) linearly onto 0–255. The sky sits at grey level 22, ±1σ of noise spans 11 to 34, a 5σ pixel is at 68, and a 50σ pixel at 148.

    The raw16 image keeps the delivered pixel values, so most of a frame sits in a narrow band near its sky level. A reader that converts 16-bit PNG files to 8 bits on loading, as OpenCV's cv2.imread does by default, leaves the sky in a few grey levels and faint sources gone; give such a reader asinh images, or load raw16 files with cv2.IMREAD_UNCHANGED.

    PyTorch¶

    skytwin.torch.detection_target turns a frame into the stretched image, scaled to 0–1, and a target with boxes (xyxy in pixels, as torchvision detectors take, or cxcywh as fractions, as DETR takes), labels and snr. Frames hold different numbers of objects, so batch them with skytwin.torch.collate.

    Limits¶

    • Hot pixels and radiation hits are in the truth file (instrument.hot_pixels, instrument.radiation.hits) but are not exported as classes.
    • Labels give boxes and paths. There are no per-pixel masks.
    • The footprint leaves out smear and bleeding along columns. saturated marks the sources that bleed.
